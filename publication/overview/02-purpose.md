## The coordination problem

Decentralized ownership helps teams develop data products close to their business domains. It also creates a coordination problem: different teams may describe similar products using incompatible names, formats, and assumptions.

A marketplace can make those descriptions visible without making them comparable. A consumer still has to ask what a dataset means, whether an endpoint is suitable, and who is responsible for the product.

DPROD supplies common metadata for these conversations. It describes products in a machine-readable form that catalogs and other tools can exchange, while leaving organizations free to extend their descriptions with additional vocabularies.

## Shared meaning, distributed ownership

A domain team can publish its own description using the same vocabulary as another domain. The descriptions can link to shared schemas, identifiers, and policies without requiring the underlying data to be moved into one central system.

This supports data mesh implementations, internal and external marketplaces, and integration across cloud platforms. The common foundation is a description that another team or tool can interpret.

## Connect description with operations

A useful product description connects metadata to operational responsibilities: endpoint reliability, access to protected data, and the business requirements a product must satisfy. Teams manage these responsibilities through their operational systems and agreements.

Start by making the product's purpose, ownership, access points, and shared meaning explicit. This gives consumers a common foundation for asking the next questions.
